# The walkthrough

One realistic task per section of the app, in the order a CFO would meet them. The tasks are
deliberately things a CFO wants, not features the app advertises — if a section cannot do the task,
that is the finding.

Sections are reached from the tabs across the top: **Home · Ask · Market scan · Drivers · Scenarios ·
Budget outlook**, with **Scheduler** and **Data ingestion** in the header. Budget 20–30 minutes in
total; you do not have to finish every section, and abandoning one with a reason is a valid result.

---

## 1. Home — ask it something that matters

The landing page is a chat box. Ask the question you would actually ask first, in your own words.
Something like *"What's the biggest risk to next year's budget?"* or *"Why is my feed cost up on
plan?"* — but use your own phrasing, not these.

Judge: did it answer with numbers about **your** business, or with generic commentary? Did it tell
you where the numbers came from? Ask one follow-up and see whether it holds the thread. Then ask it
something it should **not** be able to answer — payroll or wage costs, for instance — and see whether
it says so or makes something up. The second answer matters more than the first.

## 2. Ask — the archive

The Ask tab is every question you've asked before. Look for the conversation you just had.

Judge: is this how you'd want to find a past answer three weeks later, mid-board-prep? What would you
search for, and is that searchable?

## 3. Market scan — the report you didn't ask for

A weekly read on what moved in your input markets. Open the latest one, or generate one if there
isn't one yet (it takes a while — wait).

Judge: is this a report you'd read on a Monday, or one you'd unsubscribe from? Is there a number in
it you would act on this week? Does it tell you what *changed since last week*, or just describe the
world?

## 4. Drivers — your cost assumptions

Your cost drivers — ingredient prices, freight, FX, energy — each with a value, a source and a date.
This is meant to be the place your budget's foundations live.

Do two things:

- Pick the driver that worries you most and find out **where its number came from and when**. Follow
  the source if there is one.
- Then set your own position: lock the assumptions you are prepared to budget on, and see what the
  app does with that.

Judge: could you sit in front of the board with this screen open? Is the provenance real — a source
you could click and check — or a label? Does anything tell you which assumptions have gone stale, and
would you notice if one had?

## 5. Scenarios — the budget round itself

The core of the product. Create a new scenario and put a real budget question to it — a cost shock
you are genuinely worried about, a volume plan you're being pushed to accept, a price increase you're
considering. State it the way you'd state it to your controller.

Then look at what came back:

- Does the **EBITDA bridge** explain the move in terms you'd use — volume, price, each cost driver,
  overheads? Do the pieces add up to the total, and would you notice if they didn't?
- Freeze the scenario as a **version** and approve it. Then compare two versions and see whether
  "what changed since last month" is answerable.
- **Export** a version — the workbook or the board pack — and judge it as the document you'd actually
  circulate. Would you send it, edit it heavily, or rebuild it yourself?

Judge hardest here. This is where the app either replaces the spreadsheet you currently maintain by
hand or doesn't.

## 6. Budget outlook — next year from your own numbers

A separate read on next year's budget, driven by figures you enter yourself rather than by the
datasets behind the rest of the app. Open it, and change one assumption you'd realistically argue
about.

Judge: is this the same job as Scenarios or a different one? If you had both, which would you open on
a Tuesday morning — and does having two ways in help you or confuse you?

## 7. Scheduler and Data ingestion — the plumbing

Two screens in the header: what the app does on its own overnight, and which datasets sit behind its
answers.

Judge quickly and be willing to dismiss them. Would you ever open these, or are they someone else's
screens shown to you? If the Scheduler is where you'd set up "tell me when chicken meal moves more
than 5%", say whether you could work out how to do that unaided.

## 8. Alerts — being told, rather than asking

If there is an alert indicator, open it and investigate one.

Judge: is this something you'd want pushed at you, and is the threshold something you'd trust? Would
you act on it, or learn to ignore it?
